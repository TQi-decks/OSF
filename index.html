Not this

04 · The Position

Every HNI in your book reads the same news. Almost none of them know what it means for them.

A budget is announced and the whole country receives identical coverage, written for a salaried audience with a home loan and an eighty thousand rupee EMI. A family with fifteen crore spread across four entities reads that coverage and learns nothing.

The gap

The news stops exactly where your value begins.

A tax notification lands and every family WhatsApp group forwards the same screenshot with the same confident, wrong interpretation. The distance between what was announced and what it actually means for a family like theirs is the entire thing you get paid for. Right now, it is completely unpublished.

The opportunity

Nobody in your market is answering the third question.

Plenty of people report the news. A few explain it. Almost nobody explains it specifically to families with real, multi-entity, multigenerational assets. That audience is small, underserved, and happens to be exactly your client list.

What Faye D’Souza did for the news, we do for money.
Not the format, the function. She did not become trusted by breaking stories. She became trusted by being the person who told you, calmly, what the thing that just happened actually means for you.

Every card answers the same three questions.

This is what turns an opinion into a system. The first two questions get you read. The third one gets you hired.

Question oneWhat happened.
The budget line, the RBI decision, the notification, the rule change. Stated plainly, in two sentences, with no drama attached to it.
Question twoWhat everyone thinks it means.
The common reading. The version circulating in the WhatsApp groups and the television panels. Naming it is what signals that you have actually been listening.
Question threeWhat it actually means for a family with real assets.
The part only the two of you can write. Sequencing, entity structure, timing, the second order effect that the headline never gets to. This is the whole product.

This is not commentary. It is a demonstration.

Every card is a free, public, thirty second version of the conversation a prospective client will eventually pay you for. Nobody has to be convinced that you are good at this. They will have already watched you do it, fifty times, before they call.

Three types of card, rotating.

Enough variety that the feed never feels mechanical, enough structure that it never feels random.

Type one

The translation.

A live event, decoded for your audience. Budget, RBI, tax, regulation. This is the bulk of the output and the reason the account gets followed.

Type two

The correction.

A widely held belief that is quietly wrong. The kind of thing you find yourself explaining to a new client in the first meeting, every single time.

Type three

The practice note.

Something only visible from inside 150 households. Not opinion, observation. These are the ones that get screenshotted and forwarded.

The caption underneath.

Same five beats every time. Two hundred words, no more.

The structure

Five beats, in order.

  • The observation. The line from the card, restated once.
  • The pattern. One anonymised case from your practice.
  • The mechanism. Why it happens, structurally.
  • The correction. What a good advisor does about it.
  • The close. One flat sentence. No call to action, ever.
The discipline

What never appears.

  • No returns claimed, projected or implied.
  • No product, fund or insurer named.
  • No client identifiable, ever, in any detail.
  • Nothing that reads as regulated advice.
  • No call to action. The absence of the ask is the signal that you are not selling.
06 · The Engine

Five minutes. One voice note. That is your entire involvement.

The reason programmes like this fail is never the strategy. It is that the founder is asked to write. So we are not asking you to write anything, at any point, for six months.

Lane one

The daily voice note.

Around 7:40 in the morning, you send one voice note. Thirty seconds to five minutes. Raw, unstructured, in whatever language you think in. Whatever you read that morning, whatever a client asked you yesterday, whatever you found yourself explaining twice last week.

Lane two

The reactive call.

On the days that actually matter, budget day, an RBI announcement, a tax notification, we call you within the hour. You talk for five minutes. It publishes the same day, while the country is still arguing about the headline. Being early is most of the authority.

25minutes a week. That is the total cost of this programme to your calendar.

We are not asking either of you to become a content creator. You already produce this material every single day, in client calls and in your own head, and it currently evaporates the moment the call ends. We are only catching it.

If a morning gets missed, nothing breaks. We keep a fortnight of evergreen material banked at all times, so a busy week never becomes a silent one.
